# Final synthesis

The robot can learn about the TurtleBot3 House by using its LiDAR sensor to detect walls and objects around it. From both maps, it can see some of the walls, rooms, and hallways. However, the gray areas mean that those spaces have not been mapped yet. If an object blocks the LiDAR sensor, the robot cannot see what is behind it, so that area might not show up on the map.When the robot bumps into walls or estimates its position incorrectly, it can create multiple lines showing the same wall in different places. This makes the map less accurate. When the robot goes back to an area it already explored, SLAM might recognize the area and correct some errors, but I cannot confirm that this happened just by looking at the maps. Mission1 took 16.05 minutes, while mission2 took 29.38 minutes. Mission1 mapped more total area, which was 137.47 m², compared to mission2's 90.05 m². However, mission2 had a higher known fraction of 39.93%, while mission1 had 30.88%. Even though mission2 had better numbers for some measurements, its map showed multiple lines and some areas of the house were missing. I would trust mission1's map more for a later robot task because it shows more of the house's layout and the rooms and hallways are easier to see. One limitation is that the maps and numbers cannot tell me the robot's exact position or guarantee that it can move safely through the whole house.
