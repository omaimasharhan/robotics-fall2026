This activity gave me a better understanding of how robots can interact with people through voice commands and shared autonomy. I learned that robots need to understand not only what a person is asking for but also when they are ready, when they want to change a request, or when they want to cancel a task. What stood out to me was how important clear communication is. For example, when I said "Bring me the cup" the robot needed clarification because I did not specify which cup. Changing the feedback to ask which cup the person wanted made the interaction clearer and helped the robot understand my request. I also learned about the differences between manual teleoperation, shared autonomy with voice, and autonomous execution. Each mode has its own advantages depending on the situation. I found it interesting that shared autonomy allows people to redirect the robot while it is performing a task. This made me think about how important it is for robots to be flexible and respond to people's changing needs. Overall, this activity made me more interested in learning how to improve human-robot interaction and build robots that can understand instructions while still acting safely and giving people control when needed.
