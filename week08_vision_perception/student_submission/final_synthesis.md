All three missions explain the concept of using visual information to make correct decisions. Even though a robot can detect an object or a line using its camera, it does not mean it will always move correctly. In mission 1 the goal was to evaluate visual evidence for the location of a mug under different conditions. We compared a classical detector with learned detection methods to evaluate their performance. The learned detector performed well under normal conditions but it did not perform as well under dim lighting and occlusion. In mission 2 the goal was to detect a colored pillar and turn the robot until the pillar appeared near the center of the camera image. The outcome showed that detecting a color does not guarantee that the robot knows the object's identity or distance. In mission 3 the goal was to adjust the robot's settings so it could follow a straight and curved path and stop when it detected the finish marker. I increased the gain to 0.15 for the practice attempt and the mean absolute image error was about 0.030. For the track attempt, I decreased the speed from 0.16 to 0.13 which reduced the mean absolute image error from 0.307 to 0.26. This showed that adjusting the settings helped the robot stay more centered, although it still struggled to follow the curves.
